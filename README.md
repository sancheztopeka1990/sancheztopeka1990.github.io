# Trainory GRC Case Study

Trainory is a fictional health and fitness technology company used to demonstrate an end-to-end governance, risk, and compliance assessment. The project connects business context, asset management, cybersecurity risk, control evaluation, assurance evidence, data analytics, automation, and executive reporting.

## Assessment objective

Evaluate Trainory's baseline risk and control environment, identify material gaps, and provide a prioritized 90-day remediation plan. The management recommendation is continued controlled operation with limited conditional vendor integration while urgent access, vendor, incident-response, and recovery issues are addressed.

## Assessment results

- 15 risks assessed, including 11 rated High after existing controls
- 50% weighted maturity across 22 selected NIST CSF 2.0 outcomes
- 11 access exceptions consolidated into eight findings
- Six material findings in the VitaSync vendor assessment
- Approximately 28% modeled aggregate risk reduction from planned treatments

## Method

1. Defined the business services, data, stakeholders, systems, dependencies, and assessment boundary.
2. Connected assets to threats, vulnerabilities, business impacts, existing controls, owners, and treatment actions.
3. Compared inherent and residual risk using a consistent scoring method.
4. Evaluated selected NIST CSF 2.0 outcomes and documented evidence gaps.
5. Assessed access governance, incident readiness, and third-party risk.
6. Used Excel to aggregate detailed records into management metrics and charts.
7. Used Python to validate risk data and generate prioritized CSV and HTML reports.
8. Produced an executive conclusion and sequenced remediation roadmap.

Risk scores support prioritization and do not replace professional judgment. Documented policies and plans establish control design; operating effectiveness requires implementation, testing, and retained evidence.

## Run the Python tool

Download and extract `Trainory_Python_Risk_Scoring_Tool.zip`, then run:

```bash
python risk_tool.py sample_risks.csv
```

Windows users can run `run_demo.bat` instead.
