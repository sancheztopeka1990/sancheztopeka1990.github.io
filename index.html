Management conclusion

The strongest answer is not “fully compliant.”

The defensible conclusion is that Trainory's risk is elevated but manageable. Documentation establishes control design; operating effectiveness still requires approval, execution, testing, and retained evidence.

  • NowRemove terminated and excessive access; assign the unowned service account.
  • NextResolve critical VitaSync assurance and contract conditions.
  • ThenTest incident response, restoration, and recurring control evidence.

Interview walkthrough

Explain the project naturally in about three minutes.

  1. 0:00–0:25

    Why I built it

    “I wanted to show that I could follow risk from business context all the way to a leadership decision, using a company connected to health and fitness—something I genuinely care about.”

  2. 0:25–1:05

    How I approached risk

    Describe the asset inventory, risk register, inherent versus residual scoring, ownership, and treatment decisions. Emphasize that risk scores support judgment rather than replace it.

  3. 1:05–1:45

    How I evaluated controls

    Explain the selected NIST CSF 2.0 baseline, access review, incident plan, and VitaSync assessment. Mention the distinction between documented design and proven effectiveness.

  4. 1:45–2:25

    How I used data and code

    Show how Excel turns detailed records into leadership metrics, then explain the Python flow: CSV input → validation → scoring → prioritized CSV and HTML reports.

  5. 2:25–3:00

    What I recommended

    Close with the controlled-operation decision and 90-day roadmap: fix urgent access, strengthen vendor conditions, test response and recovery, and collect recurring evidence.

Questions to be ready for

Why did you choose this project?

Connect your interest in health and fitness with your IT education, military experience, and pivot into GRC. The subject kept the work meaningful while the control problems stayed realistic.

What part was most challenging?

Turning many detailed findings into defensible priorities without overstating compliance. Explain how evidence, ownership, and business impact shaped the recommendation.

What did the Python tool do?

It checks the CSV structure, calculates consistent inherent and residual scores, categorizes risk, sorts priorities, and produces analyst and leadership outputs.

What would you improve next?

Add evidence over time, test the incident and recovery plans, retest remediated findings, and trend risk movement across reporting periods.

Remember

You do not need to memorize every number. Know the story, your reasoning, two strong findings, and why your recommendation was defensible.
